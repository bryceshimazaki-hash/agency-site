Our Clinicians

Amy Perry

CMHC, NCC | Clinician

I specialize in working with individuals and families impacted by domestic violence, trauma, narcissistic abuse, PTSD and other kinds of abuse. With over eight years as a certified domestic violence counselor—and additional years immersed in advocacy, mentorship and support work—I bring both professional expertise and lived experience as a partner, parent, and co-parent. Clients often find that this combination allows for a deeper level of understanding, clarity, and connection as we navigate the complexities of their experiences together.

I support clients across a wide range of situations, including growing up in abusive environments, experiencing harm from a partner or parent, leaving unsafe relationships, and navigating the legal system—with or without children. My work also includes guiding clients through custody concerns, creating safety plans, and rebuilding stability and identity during and after crisis. I work closely with parents who are co-parenting with an unsafe individual, helping them develop practical skills to support and protect their children.

My approach is grounded in empowerment, honesty, and practical support. I integrate modalities such as somatic experiencing, Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT), EMDR (1.0 and 2.0), solution-focused therapy, and psychoeducation to help clients process trauma and move toward a more stable, self-directed life.

Megan Thomson

CMHC, NCC | Clinician

Therapy is often described as a difficult and raw process—and at times, it truly is. But it can also be energizing, motivating, and even enjoyable. There are moments in therapy where you may feel empowered by new insights, discovering truths about who you are and what you’re capable of becoming.

I am passionate about working with adults and teenagers (12+) who are ready to learn, heal, and grow—both in mind and body. I commonly support clients navigating PTSD, anxiety, depression, domestic violence, ADHD, trauma, OCD, and eating disorders. My approach integrates evidence-based modalities such as DBT, CBT, and ACT, along with somatic approaches and person-centered therapy.

Therapy is a collaborative process, and I strive to build a natural, authentic connection with each client. While therapy can be challenging, you don’t have to go through it alone. I provide a safe, supportive, and non-judgmental space where you can explore what matters most to you.

Eric Pribyl

LCSW | Clinician

I believe that healing from trauma isn’t about “getting over it” or “pulling yourself up by your bootstraps.” It’s about reclaiming a sense of safety in your own skin. Whether you’re navigating the complexities of domestic or intimate partner violence, processing deep-seated trauma, or looking for a space where your LGBTQIA+ identity is not just respected but truly celebrated, you don’t have to do it alone.

I work with individuals ages 7+ with a focus on supporting the queer community—especially trans and gender-diverse folks—as well as those working to stop using violence in all relationships. I specialize in working with both survivors and perpetrators of domestic violence, offering a space rooted in accountability, compassion, and real change. I also specialize in working with personality disorders, particularly BPD and NPD.

My approach is person-first and somatic-focused, meaning therapy adapts to you while honoring how your experiences live in both your mind and body. I integrate evidence-based modalities including IFS (Internal Family Systems), EMDR 2.0, mindfulness, ACT, and DBT to support lasting stability—not just survival.

Ellen Seely

CSW | Clinician

I am a Domestic Violence certified therapist who works with women, queer communities, people who use drugs, and individuals ages 16+, offering affirming, inclusive care grounded in values of empowerment, autonomy, and authenticity. With a background in public health and harm reduction, I bring specialized experience in sexual health, social justice, and supporting marginalized communities.

Over the past several years, I have taught sexual health programs, facilitated groups for queer youth, and provided compassionate, nonjudgmental support to individuals navigating stigma and complex life challenges. My approach integrates multiple modalities, including solution-focused therapy, EFT, CBT, somatic experiencing, sound healing and trauma-informed therapies.

I create spaces where clients can show up as their full selves. I am deeply committed to fostering trust, connection, and empowerment, bringing a strong dedication to advocacy and equity into my work.

Bryce Shimazaki

CSW | Clinician

I am a Domestic Violence certified therapist and social work professional with four years of experience supporting diverse populations across school, community, and domestic violence settings. I work with individuals navigating trauma, depression, anxiety, and life transitions, offering a safe, supportive space grounded in dignity, advocacy, and empowerment.

I view therapy as a collaborative partnership: You set the direction, and I provide the clinical tools to help you get there. My approach is rooted in the belief that many “struggles” began as survival skills. Together, we’ll honor how you’ve protected yourself while building new patterns that align with your values—moving from survival toward a more intentional, authentic life.

Using evidence-based practices such as DBT, ACT, CBT, mindfulness, and psychoeducation, I help clients develop emotional regulation and a stronger sense of agency. I am committed to creating a secure, respectful space where you can break cycles and move forward with confidence.

Women's Domestic Violence

This group supports women who are just learning they might be in a DV relationship, thinking about leaving, or moving forward after leaving. We identify and articulate why 'you're not crazy,' how to recognize controlling tactics, and how to use boundaries in safe ways. The ultimate goal is learning to thrive again.

"Just Breathe"

Women's Trauma Support

This group is a hybrid processing group for women who have experienced a variety of trauma. We openly discuss each other’s experiences of survival in areas of domestic violence, sexual violence, religious trauma, childhood abuse, etc. It is a safe place to receive support, give support, and share with others who understand the dynamics of trauma.
